## 1.e4 Openings

**Ruy Lopez** (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5)
- Main line: 3...a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 O-O 8.c3 d6
- Key branches: Morphy Defense (3...a6), Berlin (3...Nf6), Classical (3...Bc5), Schliemann (3...f5)
- Exchange Variation: 4.Bxc6 dxc6 — doubled pawns but bishop pair
- Marshall Attack: 8...d5 — Black sacrifices a pawn for fierce kingside attack
- Themes: central tension, slow maneuvering, pawn structures. White wants d4 break. Black wants ...d5 break.
- Beginner mistakes: Playing Bxc6 too early without purpose, not understanding a6/Ba4 tempo game

**Italian Game** (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4)
- Giuoco Piano: 3...Bc5 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb4+
- Evans Gambit: 4.b4 Bxb4 5.c3 — sacrifices pawn for rapid development + center
- Two Knights: 3...Nf6 4.d4 exd4 5.O-O (or 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Na5 — Traxler/Fried Liver territory)
- Fried Liver: 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxf7 — spectacular sacrifice, great for teaching
- Themes: open center, piece activity, tactical fireworks. More concrete than Ruy Lopez.
- Beginner mistakes: Not contesting d4, falling for Fried Liver traps

**Scotch Game** (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 exd4 4.Nxd4)
- Main: 4...Nf6 5.Nxc6 bxc6 6.e5 (or 4...Bc5 5.Be3)
- Scotch Gambit: 4.Bc4 (transposes to Italian territory)
- Themes: immediate central opening, White gets space, Black gets development
- Beginner mistakes: Recapturing with queen on d4 (gets harassed)

**King's Gambit** (1.e4 e5 2.f4)
- Accepted: 2...exf4 3.Nf3 (King's Knight Gambit) — White wants rapid development
- Declined: 2...Bc5 (Classical), 2...d5 (Falkbeer Counter-Gambit)
- Themes: romantic chess, sacrificing for attack, open f-file
- Beginner mistakes: Black holding the pawn too greedily, White forgetting king safety

**Sicilian Defense** (1.e4 c5)
- Open Sicilian: 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4
  - Najdorf: 4...Nf6 5.Nc3 a6 — most popular, flexible, fighting
  - Dragon: 4...Nf6 5.Nc3 g6 — fianchetto, Yugoslav Attack is razor-sharp
  - Scheveningen: 4...Nf6 5.Nc3 e6 — solid, small center
  - Classical: 4...Nf6 5.Nc3 Nc6 — straightforward development
- Anti-Sicilians: 2.Nc3 (Closed), 2.c3 (Alapin), 2.f4 (Grand Prix)
- Themes: asymmetric pawn structure, White has center/kingside, Black has queenside counter
- Beginner mistakes: Black playing ...d5 too early (or too late), White not knowing which plan

**French Defense** (1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5)
- Advance: 3.e5 c5 4.c3 — closed, pawn chain battle
- Exchange: 3.exd5 exd5 — symmetric, drawish
- Tarrasch: 3.Nd2 — avoids pins, solid
- Winawer: 3.Nc3 Bb4 — sharp, positional tension
- Themes: pawn chains, space vs. solidity, bad bishop on c8
- Beginner mistakes: Black not challenging the center with ...c5, White not supporting e5

**Caro-Kann** (1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5)
- Classical: 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Bf5 — solid, bishop gets out early
- Advance: 3.e5 Bf5 4.Nf3 e6 — similar to French but bishop is active
- Themes: solid structure, Black aims for ...c5 break, less cramped than French
- Beginner mistakes: White playing passively against the solid structure

**Scandinavian** (1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qa5)
- Themes: early queen development (unusual), simple development plan
- Beginner mistakes: White chasing the queen instead of developing

**Pirc/Modern** (1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6)
- Themes: hypermodern — let White build center, then undermine it
- Classical: 4.Nf3, Austrian Attack: 4.f4
- Beginner mistakes: Black being too passive, White not using center advantage

**Alekhine's Defense** (1.e4 Nf6 2.e5 Nd5 3.d4 d6)
- Themes: provocative — Black says "come get me," aims to overextend White's center
- Beginner mistakes: White pushing pawns too far, Black not timing ...d6/...c5

**Philidor Defense** (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 d6)
- Themes: solid but passive, Black supports e5 but blocks bishop
- Beginner mistakes: Black getting a cramped position

## 1.d4 Openings

**Queen's Gambit Declined** (1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6)
- Orthodox: 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 — classical setup
- Tartakower: 5...b6 — fianchetto the problem bishop
- Themes: minority attack, pawn breaks, piece pressure. Solid and strategic.
- Beginner mistakes: Capturing on c4 without a plan, not contesting the center

**Queen's Gambit Accepted** (1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4)
- Main: 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4 — White recovers the pawn easily
- Themes: Black gets free development but White gets center
- Beginner mistakes: Black trying to hold the c4 pawn

**Slav Defense** (1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6)
- Main: 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 dxc4 — Black takes and develops the bishop
- Semi-Slav: 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 e6 — Meran Variation is sharp
- Themes: solid pawn chain, bishop can develop to f5 (unlike QGD)
- Beginner mistakes: Not knowing when to take on c4

**King's Indian Defense** (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6)
- Classical: 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Be2 e5 — the main battleground
- Sämisch: 5.f3 — fortress setup, bayonet attack
- Themes: Black allows White a big center then attacks it with ...e5 or ...c5.
- Beginner mistakes: Black not playing ...e5 in time, White not using queenside space

**Nimzo-Indian** (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4)
- Classical: 4.Qc2, Rubinstein: 4.e3, Kasparov: 4.Nf3
- Themes: piece pressure over center, doubled c-pawns if Bxc3
- Beginner mistakes: White not knowing how to handle doubled pawns

**Grünfeld Defense** (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 d5)
- Exchange: 4.cxd5 Nxd5 5.e4 Nxc3 6.bxc3 Bg7 — Black attacks White's center
- Themes: hypermodern — Black gives White the center and destroys it.
- Beginner mistakes: White not building the center aggressively enough

**Dutch Defense** (1.d4 f5)
- Stonewall: ...f5, ...d5, ...e6, ...Bd6 — rigid but solid
- Leningrad: ...f5, ...g6, ...Bg7 — dragon-like setup
- Themes: kingside attack for Black, weakened king position
- Beginner mistakes: Black weakening the king too much

**Benoni** (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 c5 3.d5 e6 4.Nc3 exd5 5.cxd5 d6)
- Themes: asymmetric pawn structure, Black wants ...b5 break
- Beginner mistakes: Black being too passive on queenside

**Budapest Gambit** (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5)
- Themes: surprise weapon, Black gets activity for the pawn
- Beginner mistakes: White not knowing the theory

## 1.c4 / 1.Nf3

**English Opening** (1.c4)
- Symmetrical: 1...c5 — can transpose to many structures
- Reversed Sicilian: 1...e5 — White plays a Sicilian with an extra tempo
- Themes: flexible, transpositional, flank control

**Réti Opening** (1.Nf3 d5 2.c4)
- Themes: hypermodern, control center from flanks, flexible development

**London System** (1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 e6 3.Bf4)
- Themes: solid system for White, simple development plan, avoids theory
- Beginner mistakes: Black not challenging with ...c5 early
