## Ep 4 — The Immortal Game (Anderssen–Kieseritzky, London 1851)
**Star idea:** activity beats material — give up everything, keep the initiative.
**Hook flavor:** the sacrifice body-count keeps climbing.

- **setup1** — London, eighteen fifty-one. Adolf Anderssen has the white pieces, in a game so famous it earned a name: the Immortal.
- **setup2** — Black keeps grabbing material. Anderssen never stops to take anything back.
- **build1** — He plays for one thing only: every piece pointing at the black king.
- **sac1** — Bishop to d6, ignoring his own rook. Black takes the rook. Anderssen just pushes a pawn.
- **sac2** — Now the queen sits in. Black's queen roams to a1 and swallows the other rook, far from home.
- **turn1** — Black is up a queen and two rooks. It looks completely lost for White.
- **star1** — Then the knight strikes. Knight takes g7, the king is chased to d8, and the quiet move arrives.
- **star2** — Queen to f6. A queen sacrifice. Black is forced to take it with the knight.
- **mate1** — And the bishop finishes. Bishop e7, checkmate — with almost nothing left on the board.
- **idea1** — That is the lesson. Material is paper if your pieces do not work. Activity is the real king.
- **tag1** — He gave away a queen and two rooks and mated with a bishop. That is why they call it Immortal.

## Ep 5 — Marshall's Golden Queen (Levitsky–Marshall, Breslau 1912)
**Star idea:** a sacrifice is about the squares it opens, not the piece.
**Hook flavor:** one move, offered three ways, unbeatable every way.

- **hook1** — Black puts his queen on a square three different white pieces can capture. Every single capture loses.
- **setup1** — Breslau, nineteen twelve. Frank Marshall, Black, has White on the ropes and finds a move people still talk about.
- **setup2** — The move is queen to g3. It sits right in front of the white king, defended by nothing.
- **q1** — A pawn can take it. The other pawn can take it. The queen can take it. Three ways to accept.
- **ref1** — If the h-pawn takes, knight to e2 is checkmate.
- **ref2** — If the f-pawn takes, knight e2, then rook takes f1 — checkmate.
- **ref3** — And if the queen takes, knight e2 forks king and queen. Black wins the queen and the game.
- **turn1** — Three captures, three disasters. So White did the only sane thing left. White resigned.
- **idea1** — The idea: a sacrifice is not about the piece. It is about the squares the piece opens.
- **legend1** — As the story goes, the crowd was so stunned they showered the board with gold coins.
- **tag1** — One move, offered three ways, unbeatable every way. The golden queen.

- **star2** — Queen to g8. A queen sacrifice, right beside the king. The rook is forced to take it.
- **mate1** — And the knight returns. Knight f7, checkmate. The king is smothered by its own rook and pawns.
- **idea1** — That is the whole trick: make the enemy's pieces the cage, and let the knight turn the key.
- **tag1** — A queen thrown away so a knight can finish. The smothered mate.
